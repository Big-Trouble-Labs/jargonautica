# Children and AI

## Plain View

The particular considerations when the user might be a child. Age-appropriate content is the obvious one. Less obvious are the effects on learning when a tool can do the homework, the durability of relationships formed with a system, and the difficulty of consent when the user cannot meaningfully give it.

## Technical View

Regulatory hooks include children's data codes requiring high-privacy defaults and age-appropriate design, plus specific provisions in online safety legislation. Practical measures include age assurance proportionate to risk, restricted content and memory settings for minors, crisis escalation routes, and parental transparency. Educational evidence suggests assistance can support or substitute for learning depending on task design, with unstructured use tending towards substitution.
