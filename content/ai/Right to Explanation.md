# Right to Explanation

## Plain View

The idea that someone affected by an automated decision should be told why. What that requires in practice is contested, and a model that is not interpretable cannot supply a mechanistic account. In practice explanation usually means describing what information was used, what the decision process was, and how to challenge it.

## Technical View

The legal position rests on rights to meaningful information about the logic involved rather than a technical exposition. Practical approaches include documenting inputs and decision criteria, providing counterfactual explanations stating what would need to differ for a different outcome, and local attribution methods, all of which have known limitations regarding faithfulness. Counterfactual explanations are often the most useful and actionable form for the individual.
